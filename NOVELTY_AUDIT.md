# NOVELTY AUDIT

Prompt rule: never claim "nobody studied SES × refugee distance". Checked
against the literature map (LITERATURE_MAP.csv) — audit dimensions:

## 1. SES ↔ individual migration distance, direct link

- Closest identified: Kohlenberger/Buber-Ennser et al. (PLOS ONE 2023) — Kraków
  vs Vienna convenience samples; shows farther-west movers are more positively
  selected (education, employment, urbanity). Two-point host comparison,
  no distance distribution, no mixture.
- Aksoy, Poutvaara & Schikora (CESifo WP 2020) — multi-destination
  self-selection on education for 2015–16 arrivals; destination treated as
  discrete choice, not distance distribution.
- Brücker et al. 2023 — Germany-only profile.

Gap: continuous individual-level distance distribution over ~85 destinations
and its decomposition. Positioned as extension, not contradiction: our
selection-reversal arm (F) generalizes their two-point finding.

## 2. Distance distribution modelled as a mixture

No forced-migration study found fitting finite-mixture / mixture-of-experts
models to individual migration distance. Latent-class destination *choice*
(Greene-Hensher tradition) exists in transport/economic migration contexts but
not in refugee-distance work. → strongest novelty leg, keep the claim narrow:
"first finite-mixture characterization of war-refugee distance" (verified only
against surveyed literature; systematic review required before submission).

## 3. Capacity vs attachment as competing mechanisms

Components exist separately: aspirations-capabilities (de Haas 2021),
trapped populations (Carling 2002; Foresight 2011), location-specific capital
(DaVanzo 1981), place attachment (Lewicka 2011). Joint operationalization as
two rival distance-generating regimes within one war — not found. Framing
connects to existing concepts; no new term is coined ("dual mechanism" used
descriptively).

## 4. Selection reversing the SES–distance relation

Positive self-selection of refugees on education documented (L05, L06). The
specific reversal claim — poorest under-represented among crossers, yet the
observed low-SES crossers skew far — is testable in our stage design and not
identified in prior work. Retain as hypothesis F, not claim.

## 5. Overlap with onward-migration literature

Onward/secondary migration (Toma & Castagnone 2015) studies post-arrival
moves; our primary outcome is first-arrival distance; onward arm is
secondary and explicitly cross-referenced.

## New-risk flags

- A systematic database search (Scopus/WoS) is still required before any
  submission; this audit covers named literatures, not exhaustive coverage.
- If a prior mixture-modelling refugee study exists, contribution degrades to
  replication+extension — still publishable, must be stated honestly.
